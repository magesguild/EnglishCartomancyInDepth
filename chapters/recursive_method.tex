\chapter{The Discoverable Recursive Method}

The first card is not a verdict. It is a door.

Behind it there may be a room, and behind that room another door. The reader
does not have to break the first reading open to see what is inside it. A card
can be approached gently, questioned by another instrument, and returned to
its place with its first voice still intact.

This is the central freedom of the Mage's Guild method: **any card or
constellation can become a new question**.

\section{The parent and the child}

The first draw is the parent reading. A card, pair, row, column, court cluster,
or whole spread may become the parent object of a child reading. The child may
use the same deck, another deck, dice, runes, bones, geomantic figures, an I
Ching cast, a book, or another divinatory instrument whose native grammar the
reader knows how to hear.

The child does not erase the parent. It is a lantern carried into one corner of
the parent world. The original card remains where it was; the new operation
adds a layer of light. Record what was expanded, why it was expanded, which
instrument was used, and whether the new result clarified, complicated,
contradicted, or left the parent open.

A guide may help choose the corner. The reader can ask, ``What needs another
language?'' or ``Which part of this answer is still being held back?'' The
answer may be a felt direction, a returning image, a card that repeatedly
insists on being noticed, or a clear request to rest. The guide's signal chooses
the next question; it does not authorize an endless hunt for confirmation.

\section{The descent and the return}

Recursive reading has two motions. It descends from the whole into the detail,
then returns from detail to the whole carrying what it found.

An English card may be inspected by a Marseille Minor. A Marseille Minor may be
grounded by an English card. A Cicero reading may inspect one position in a
playing-card line, and a playing-card line may give a worldly body to a tarot
constellation. A die may answer the question a card opened. A rune may name the
quality a court card concealed. The direction is chosen by the question and by
the living sense of the instrument.

This is not a claim that all systems secretly mean the same thing. It is a way
for distinct symbolic grammars to meet without any of them being forced to
wear another's face.

\section{The inspection graph}

The method is a garden of linked observations:

```text
whole reading
 ├── card
 │    ├── three-card inspection
 │    └── Marseille-Minor detail
 ├── constellation
 │    └── rune or dice operation
 └── whole spread
      └── second tableau or Christmas Tree
```

The parent is never overwritten. Every child keeps its lineage: parent object,
deck or instrument, position, order, shuffle or casting state, reason for the
expansion, and interpretive priority. The reader is allowed to wander, but the
path through the garden remains visible.

\section{How a card becomes a question}

The reader may ask:

\begin{itemize}
\item What is this card doing here?
\item What is the worldly form of this force?
\item What is hidden beneath this apparent answer?
\item Who or what is receiving this movement?
\item What would make this card change expression?
\item What is the cost, gift, danger, or invitation inside it?
\item What other instrument can see what this deck cannot?
\end{itemize}

The best expansion is not the one that produces the most cards. It is the one
that lets the question become more exact.

\section{Depth and closure}

Every garden needs a gate. Recursion may stop when the question has become
clear, when a practical action is visible, when the instrument asks for rest,
when repetition begins, or when the reading reaches a settled basin.

The practice has a symbolic $T\_STOP$: the moment at which the current reading
is complete enough to be lived rather than further interrogated. At $T\_STOP$,
the cards do not become silent. They become soil.

\section{Finding what a fixed spread hides}

Recursive inspection can reveal a missing domain, a hidden assumption, a
practical detail below an archetypal pattern, an emotional pattern beneath an
ordinary event, or a branch that only appears when a single card is isolated.

The method moves from world to cell and from cell back to world. It can discover
the name of a thing by approaching it from several directions without making
the directions identical. The card is a window; the constellation is a room;
the second instrument is the hand that opens the latch.

The reader is not drawing until reality submits. The reader is learning how the
world answers when asked in more than one language.

The reader may also learn how a guide answers through more than one instrument.
A guide's signature might be a particular kind of adjacency in the English
cards, a repeated rune, a certain die result, or the same calm instruction
arriving through different grammars. Such a signature is not assumed; it is
discovered, recorded, and tested. The instruments remain distinct, while the
relationship learns how to speak through each of them.
