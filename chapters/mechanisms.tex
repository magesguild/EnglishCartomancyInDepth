\chapter{Mechanisms, Flow, and Pairings}

The English system becomes most informative when it is read as a flow rather
than as a row of isolated definitions.

\section{Suit as domain}

Hearts describe emotional and personal life; Diamonds describe material,
communicative, and practical affairs; Clubs describe effort, enterprise, and
growth through action; Spades describe challenge, consequence, cost, conflict,
and necessary endings. A suit is a domain, not a moral verdict.

\section{Number as operation}

The number describes the operation taking place in its suit. The working
sequence is: maximum force (Ace), duality (Two), multiplication (Three),
consolidation (Four), disruption (Five), gradual resolution (Six), obstacle
(Seven), movement and arrival (Eight), intensity near completion (Nine), and
completion (Ten).

Repeated numbers amplify an operation across domains. Repeated suits establish
the reading's center of gravity. A number and a suit must be read together;
neither is a complete statement by itself.

\section{Flow}

A line can be read as force passing, force present, and force approaching. It
can also be read as origin, development, complication, response, and result.
These are not mandatory labels. They are ways of watching a state move.

Movement can be caused by a card, a neighboring card, a suit transition, a
repeated number, or a court acting upon a number card. The reader should ask:
what is moving, what is receiving it, and what changes because they meet?

\section{Pairings}

Pairings are not a closed list of secret combinations. They are relations that
become reliable through repeated observation. Important pair types include:

\begin{itemize}
\item same-number pairs across different domains;
\item adjacent cards of the same suit;
\item a court beside a number card of its suit;
\item a favorable card beside a cost-bearing Spade;
\item two cards that repeat or oppose one another's operation;
\item a card whose meaning changes when it enters a new position.
\end{itemize}

Record a pairing as a claim with a context, not as an eternal law. A pairing
becomes part of the working vocabulary when it repeatedly clarifies rather
than merely resembles later events.

\section{Constellations}

A constellation may be two adjacent cards, a row, a column, a court cluster, a
repeated number, or the entire spread. A local constellation can be inspected
without losing the whole reading. The whole reading can also be inspected as a
single object.
