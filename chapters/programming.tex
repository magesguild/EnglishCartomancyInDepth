\chapter{Programming Card Sets}

The card practice can be used not only to read a state but to author a symbolic
configuration. An ordered card set is a stored program: its order, positions,
attention, and intended path form a state that can later be executed by dealing
from the top.

\section{Authoring}

The reader may select a complete deck or a focused partial set, arrange cards
in a chosen positional grammar, pray or meditate over the arrangement, and
store it without disturbing the order. The arrangement is reversible: an old
stack can be restored, a new stack can be made as a branch, and a minor card can
be replaced without rewriting the entire positional architecture.

\section{LIFO execution}

The stored arrangement is a last-in, first-out stack. The top card is met first;
successive cards are exposed by popping downward through the preserved order.
The act of dealing is the execution trace. Shuffling is entropy injection and
therefore breaks the prior program rather than reproducing it.

\section{Charging and boundaries}

Prayer, meditation, attention, and intention are part of the practice's
initialization. The Hearth Compact remains the safety boundary: play, consent,
harm reduction, respect, patience, and freedom. Programming a desired path
does not authorize overriding another person's will. Its most reliable worldly
path is through attention, preparation, recognition, decision, and action.

\section{Forth and khipu}

The method is closer to Forth or khipu than to a closed ceremonial machine.
Small operations compose. The reader discovers the grammar by using it. A
question can call an expansion; a card can be passed to another instrument; a
stored arrangement carries lineage and can be revised. This is an architectural
analogy, not a claim about historical origins.
