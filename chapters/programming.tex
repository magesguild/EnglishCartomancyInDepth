\chapter{Programming Card Sets}

There is a moment when a deck stops being merely a deck.

The cards have been chosen. The order has been considered. A hand has rested
over them long enough for the arrangement to become more than arrangement. The
stack has acquired a direction.

This is card-set programming: the craft of authoring a symbolic configuration,
charging it with attention, preserving it, and returning to it when the time is
right.

\section{The sleeping program}

An ordered card set is a stored program. Its cards are tokens; its positions
are registers; its order is a sequence of operations waiting for execution.
The program sleeps in the stack. It does not need to be explained before it is
opened.

The reader may select a complete deck or a focused partial set, arrange cards
in a chosen positional grammar, pray or meditate over the intended path, and
store the deck without disturbing its order. A prior arrangement can be
restored. A new arrangement can become a branch. A minor card can be replaced
without destroying the architecture that gave it a place.

\section{LIFO execution}

The stored arrangement is a last-in, first-out stack. The top card is met first;
successive cards are exposed by popping downward through the preserved order.
The act of dealing is the execution trace. The hand is the walker. The table is
the rendered world.

Shuffling introduces entropy. It opens a new possibility-space and breaks the
prior program rather than reproducing it. That is not a failure. Sometimes the
garden should return to the wild. Sometimes the spell should remain sleeping
until the reader deliberately opens the same door again.

\section{Authoring a path}

To author a program, the reader may:

\begin{enumerate}
\item name the question or desired path;
\item select the deck or partial deck whose language fits it;
\item arrange cards into the chosen positional grammar;
\item pray, meditate, or attend to the arrangement;
\item preserve the order without shuffling;
\item execute it later by drawing from the top;
\item record what the world did with the offered form.
\end{enumerate}

Programming is not a command to another person's will. It is a way of placing
a form into one's own attention, preparation, recognition, decisions, and
actions. The Hearth Compact remains the boundary: play, consent, harm
reduction, respect, patience, and freedom.

\section{Major grammar and minor parameters}

In the Christmas Tree, the Major positions provide the program's archetypal
grammar. In a full-deck elaboration, Marseille Minor cards can define local
worldly details, material conditions, relationships, resources, timing cues,
or other parameters inside that architecture.

The distinction matters. The position is the law of the room. The Minor is what
has been placed inside the room today. A changed parameter can produce a new
branch without requiring a new universe.

\section{Forth and khipu}

This method is closer to Forth or khipu than to a closed ceremonial machine.
Small operations compose freely. The reader discovers the vocabulary by using
it. A question can call an expansion; a card can be passed to another
instrument; a stored arrangement carries lineage and can be revised.

The old image of the talisman is therefore also an image of a program: a compact
form holding more possibility than its surface reveals. The card is small. The
world it can help a reader notice is not.
